Speaker notes

I owned this account creation workflow from 2020 to 2026. My responsibility began with Cash App and broadened across Block. I worked with engineering through successive improvements, including Launchpad 2.0, scheduled processing, Okta activation, and support enablement.

The workforce grew from approximately 4,000 full time employees to a peak of approximately 12,000. Including our outsourced customer support teams, referred to as BPO, the total workforce exceeded twice that FTE peak. BPO hiring continued to grow, with cohorts of 30 to 60 people and multiple waves each week.

Weekly routine account creation effort fell from approximately 20 hours to less than one as the workflow matured.

Speaker notes

Each office originally created accounts and handed new hires printed temporary passwords and instructions. Remote onboarding required us to create accounts and deliver login details to people joining from different locations, while hiring continued at high volume.

We centralized account creation within a three person team. As Cash App's first dedicated IT support person, I owned its account creation work, including large BPO cohorts. My responsibilities broadened across Block as the process evolved.

Centralizing the work kept onboarding moving and gave us a shared understanding of the recurring problems to address with engineering.

Speaker notes

Workday reports supplied the records. I copied names into a Ruby command line script that connected through an API to Registry, our LDAP directory. The workflow created the identity and Google account, using options for employees, vendors, contractors, and BPO workers.

I resolved username and full name conflicts, selected usernames when needed, and reran failed records. For remote onboarding, I also prepared individual temporary password PDFs in Drive. Support staff retrieved each document and verified the person's identity before sharing credentials.

The repeated input, supervision, corrections, and credential handling made account creation a substantial weekly task.

Speaker notes

The engineer most familiar with the Ruby script left the company. Employee Experience still owned it, but another engineer often had to learn the script before helping us.

I put a wrapper around the existing script that looped through the rows in a CSV. It ran the script for each person until the file was complete, then returned output showing what had completed and which records needed attention.

This let us prepare a cohort from the Workday report, run the batch, and review the results. The wrapper reused the existing account creation logic. Naming conflicts and credential delivery remained the next areas to improve.

Speaker notes

I partnered with Employee Experience engineers to bring the workflow into Launchpad, a GUI fed directly from Workday. Upcoming hires were already visible in the interface, removing the need to copy records into the script.

Starting multiple accounts at the same time could cause race conditions. I started one or two records, waited for completion, and checked the results before continuing. Naming failures still required manual review.

The interface reduced data entry, while I continued to supervise execution. That remaining work led to the next improvement with engineering: batch execution.

Speaker notes

I partnered with engineering on a feature called “Run All.” One action started processing for an entire cohort, and I reviewed and corrected the errors returned.

The workflow also generated and saved onboarding PDFs automatically. Support staff used those documents during remote onboarding.

At this stage, I still initiated each batch. My attention shifted to the records needing correction, and the next improvement was to schedule creation around each new hire's start date.

Speaker notes

When a new software engineering team took over Launchpad, I explained how the existing systems connected, the recurring naming issues, onboarding timing, and the steps that still needed manual attention. We used that operating experience to shape Launchpad 2.0 together.

Routine account creation ran before the start date. Improved naming logic reduced manual username corrections.

Workday remained the source of truth. Launchpad created the identity in Registry, our LDAP directory. Identity information then passed to Okta, which handled Google Workspace and other configured application provisioning.

Speaker notes

Earlier, support retrieved temporary password PDFs from Drive, verified each new hire's identity, and shared the credentials. This remained part of onboarding after PDF generation and storage were automated.

With Okta, the new hire received an activation link and set their own password.

Later additions sent invitations to People onboarding meetings and optional IT office hours, connecting new hires with the sessions and support available to them.

Speaker notes

A Cash App team was entering information into Workday incorrectly, which affected my account creation work.

I spoke directly with a peer about how the process needed to work and explained the downstream impact. It was a difficult feedback conversation with a positive outcome.

Workday was our source of truth, so reliable onboarding depended on both the source team and IT support. Addressing that relationship was part of owning the workflow.

Speaker notes

As routine processing became automated, I owned and curated the runbooks so support staff could understand the workflow, identify failures, and know what to check next.

I trained the global, East Coast, and West Coast IT support teams directly. I made sure individuals felt comfortable with the process and gave them access to investigate and resolve account errors.

Documentation, training, and tool access gave the teams the ability to help new hires directly. I continued partnering with support and engineering to keep the workflow useful and reliable.

Speaker notes

Weekly routine effort fell from approximately 20 hours to less than one as the workflow evolved.

CSV batching removed repeated data entry. The GUI brought Workday records into the interface. Run All replaced individual starts with one batch action. Scheduled processing automated batch initiation. Improved naming logic reduced corrections, and Okta activation replaced temporary password delivery.

My focus shifted toward improving the workflow, resolving exceptions, and enabling support teams. That progress came through sustained partnership with engineering and IT support.

Speaker notes

The pattern I carry forward is to stay close to the work, identify the next constraint, and build improvements with the people who use and maintain the workflow.

After the original Ruby expert left, engineers often had to learn the script before they could help. I would work with engineering earlier to establish a backup technical owner and keep the tool documentation current alongside the operational runbooks.

Ownership includes building the knowledge and partnerships that keep a workflow supportable when people or teams change. The runbooks, direct training, and support access later in the project put that principle into practice.
